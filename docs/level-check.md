# Level check

*Ten minutes of play, spread over two or three days. Nothing here is a test for
your child. It is a test of where the binder should start.*

Answer the parent questions in a few words each. For the play checks, write down
what your child did, including "wouldn't do it". "Wouldn't do it" is useful
information, not a fail.

Your answers are about your child, so keep them out of a shared repository. A note
on your phone or a private file is fine.

## Part 1: parent questions (answer from memory)

1. How high can they count out loud without help? Where does it fall apart?
2. If you put 5 dinos in front of them and say "how many?", do they touch each one
   and land on "five", or say a string of numbers?
3. Do they recognise any written numerals? Which ones?
4. Which letters do they name on sight, including the letters of their own name? Do
   they know any letter *sounds* (say "sss" for S, not "ess")?
5. Have they ever noticed a letter in the wild ("that's my letter!") without being
   prompted?
6. Which dinosaurs can they name from their toys or books right now? Do they
   already know that some eat meat?
7. What is their longest stretch on a self-chosen activity: 2 minutes, 5, 10, more?
   Which activity?
8. Can they draw a circle? A line between two things? Do they hold a crayon in a
   fist or with their fingers?
9. Do they do puzzles? Chunky peg puzzles, or 12-piece jigsaw-style?
10. Do they fill in the next word of a familiar book? Retell any story on their own?
11. Rhymes: do they ever notice or make rhymes ("cat, hat, bat")?
12. Dot matching: with a few cards of 1 to 5 dots, do they match dots to dots, or
    dots to a written number? Up to which number does it work?
13. What frustrates them fastest: sitting, being shown how, being wrong, or waiting?

## Part 2: play checks (one or two per day, stop the moment it stops being fun)

**A. Give me N.** Pile of 8 dinos or blocks. "Give me three." Then "give me five."
Then "give me two."
Watch for: do they count out the exact number and stop, or hand over a handful?
Stopping at the right number is cardinality, and it decides whether number work
starts at 3–4 or at 5.

**B. Flash the dots.** Draw 2 dots on a card, 3 on another, 4 on another. Show one
for a second and hide it. "How many?"
Watch for: which ones they get *without* counting. That is subitizing. Most
three-year-olds have 2, some have 3.

**C. More or fewer.** Two plates: 4 grapes and 6 grapes. "Which plate has more?"
Then 3 vs 4.
Watch for: do they just look, or count to check when it is close? Counting to check
is the better answer.

**D. Letter sounds.** Write S, T, A, P, I, N big on paper. Point to each: "what's
this one? what does it say?"
Watch for: names, sounds, or nothing, per letter. Write down which for each.

**E. First sounds.** Say slowly: "sssun. What sound does sun start with?" Try
*mmmoon*, *tttop*.
Watch for: any first sound caught means blending can start early.

**F. Blend a word.** Say "c… a… t. What word did I say?" Try "s… u… n" and
"d… o… g".
Watch for: this is the single best predictor of early reading. If they get one,
say so loudly; it changes the plan.

**G. Pattern.** Line up red-blue-red-blue magnet tiles. Hand them a red and a blue.
"What comes next?"
Watch for: the correct next tile, or a random tile. Then try red-red-blue.

**H. What's missing.** Four dinos in a row; they name them. Cover the row with a
towel, remove one. "Who ran away?"
Watch for: naming the missing one, or needing the row uncovered.

**I. Draw the line.** Two dinos on the left of a page, two matching eggs on the
right, mixed up. "Draw a line from each dino to its egg."
Watch for: whether they can draw a controlled line at all. This decides
draw-the-line pages versus pair-the-cards.

## What to write down

The 13 answers and a line per check, A through I. Also note which toy dinosaurs
you have at home, so the weekly dinosaur can be one your child can hold.

## How the answers change the binder

Unit 1 as shipped assumes a starting point of: counts 1–4 objects with meaning, no
letter sounds yet, cannot yet draw a closed circle, and shuts down when made to
"do the page". If your child is past that:

- **A stops at five** → start number play at 5 instead of 3–4.
- **B sees three without counting** → put numerals on the match cards from week 1.
- **E or F works** → blending can start in Unit 2 instead of Unit 3.
- **I works** → draw-the-line pages are fine; the kits use pair-the-cards until
  then.
- **They sit happily for ten minutes** → keep the invitations, but the harder rung
  on each card can be the default.
