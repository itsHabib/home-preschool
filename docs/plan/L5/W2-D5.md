# W2-D5 Triceratops has dinner
Parent: W2 — repeat /t/ and round play through an adult-read eating-scene mini-book.
Serves: R1 R2 L1 M0
Goal: Invite the child to share a Triceratops dinner story, hear /t/, and make a dough loop or free circle.
- Template: mini-book; portrait US Letter with four adult-cut panels numbered 1–4. Panel 1: dinosaur portrait, name and isolated T. Panel 2: dinosaur with a leaf visibly at its mouth, caption “It eats plants.” Panel 3: isolated T, familiar towel/sock pictures and adult-read question “Which starts with /t/?” Panel 4: a small dinosaur, “A round resting place,” and blank play space. This is a short shared activity book, not a four-event story or a decodable book. The child's name appears in each panel. Parent reads all captions. No written picture labels, tracing guides or required letter formation. Footer gives decision and extension.
- Decision the child makes: Which picture word starts with /t/: towel or sock?
- Invitation (two alternatives): "Want to turn the pages while I read, or make a dough dinner space while I tell the story?" Neither route requires sitting, finishing, repeating captions, or answering.
- Setup/play: Cut the four panels and stack 1–4. Set out dough, a mat, crayons and scrap paper. Read the captions as they turn pages; they are not expected to read them. Talk about the dinosaur eating plants. Name “towel” and “sock” naturally before asking which starts with /t/. Show T and model the sound. On the last page, they can join a dough snake into a resting loop, or make their own crayon circle on scrap. No need to do every part. If they want a repeat, reverse the spoken word order.
- Harder rung: Cover the pictures and invite the sound for T, if they want another turn.
- Materials: One page and parent card, adult scissors, optional clip/ring, dough, washable mat or protector, crayons and scrap paper; parent present. Printed sound pictures supply the choices; real objects are not required.
Exit check: Optional two-minute glimpse during willing play. The card asks: “Did they make a crayon circle with the ends meeting?” Notice only a circle they choose to make; do not add a task to collect a result. Dough loops and finger rounds are practice, not M0 attainment. Adult reading or echoed sounds do not establish independent reading. Declining is fine.
Children:
Constraints inherited:
- Starting calibration, from the first level check: "Counting things | 1–4 with meaning"; "Letters | Does not recognise letters yet, including their own name"; "Drawing | Cannot draw a circle yet"; "Temperament | Frustrated when they feel forced".
- Numbers start with 3–4 objects, stretch to 5 only when ready; dot-to-dot matching starts at 3 then 4. Numerals ride along from W1, never required answers. Subitizing and play checks A, B, D, G, I remain unknown.
- Every activity lasts 2–10 minutes, hands first; no pencils, sitting lessons or draw-the-line tasks before M1. Per the parent level check, playdough, magnet tiles, Lego, crayons, cut cards, clips and rings are fine with a parent present.
- Set it out and play near it. Every card offers two invitations; the child may decline both. Stop immediately on pushback. Never move their hand, supply the answer, or require finishing.
- One printable page and one parent card each day. Name the actual learning decision and a conditional harder rung on both. An invitation choice alone is not the learning decision.
- Synthetic phonics uses sounds, not letter names: initial set s a t p i n; U1's specified dinosaur order is S, T, A. No other taught graphemes yet. Oral blending comes before letter-tile words; no CVC tile reading or tricky words in U1. Tricky words start at R4, two at a time.
- One focal dinosaur per week; its initial is the week's sound. All pages carry the child's name for optional finger exploration, without name-letter testing. No letter formation requirement.
- Diet, era and feature claims need two sources. The weekly dinosaur is the star; the three U1 dinosaurs and T. rex may supply contrasts. No invented colors, plate purpose, or claim these dinosaurs lived together.
- M0 opportunities include dough loops and free crayon circles on scrap paper. Dough and finger play are practice; only an independent rough crayon circle meets M0.
- Exit checks are optional two-minute glimpses during play. Parent cards ask one plain question about what happened; declining is fine and never failure.
- Focal dinosaur and sound: Triceratops; T /t/ (a clipped sound, no added uh). Facts to verify before build: Three horns and a frill; Late Cretaceous; plants.
Open questions:
- ASSUMED: first-sound readiness is still unknown until observed; repeat the simple oral invitation without presuming D1 success. Dinosaur diet and eating-scene details require the parent build pass's two-source fact check.
