# W2-D1 Triceratops and a dough loop
Parent: W2 — offer /t/ noticing and round play around the coloring page.
Serves: R1 R2 M0
Goal: Invite the child to hear /t/ and make round movements while meeting Triceratops.
- Template: coloring-page. One full-page Triceratops line drawing with space for free coloring, isolated T, and a narrow adult-read fact strip: "Triceratops • try-SAIR-uh-tops • plants • three horns and a frill • Late Cretaceous." Include the child's name for optional finger exploration. No sound-choice pictures, dotted letters, or tracing paths. One parent card carries the oral sound choice, play routes, decision, extension, and observation question; repeat decision and harder rung in an adult-facing page footer.
- Decision the child makes: Which spoken word starts with /t/: sock or towel?
- Invitation (two alternatives): "Want to make a dough loop for Triceratops, or color them while I tell you about them?" They can choose either or neither; neither requires finishing the page.
- Setup/play: The coloring page, crayons and scrap paper; a little dough on a washable mat. Share the dinosaur strip as you play. Let them color or join the ends of a dough snake. A free crayon circle on scrap is welcome too. For a sound turn, say “sock” and “towel” naturally; ask which starts with /t/. If the words or game are new, show one together first. Point to T and model /t/; an echo is welcome. If they want a repeat, reverse the spoken word order.
- Harder rung: If that sound game is easy, try a fresh spoken pair: sun or tap.
- Materials: One coloring page and one parent card; playdough and a washable work surface; scrap paper; fat crayons; one towel and one sock. Parent present for all materials.
Exit check: An optional two-minute glimpse during play: swap the towel and sock positions and offer the oral /t/ choice once, without a cue. On another willing occasion, notice whether a free crayon circle closes; dough/finger loops are practice only. Card question: "What did the child choose or make on their own today?" A brief word or description is enough; declining is fine, and one sound choice does not establish R2.
Children:
Constraints inherited:
- Starting calibration, from the first level check: "Counting things | 1–4 with meaning"; "Letters | Does not recognise letters yet, including their own name"; "Drawing | Cannot draw a circle yet"; "Temperament | Frustrated when they feel forced".
- Numbers start with 3–4 objects, stretch to 5 only when ready; dot-to-dot matching starts at 3 then 4. Numerals ride along from W1, never required answers. Subitizing and play checks A, B, D, G, I remain unknown.
- Every activity lasts 2–10 minutes, hands first; no pencils, sitting lessons or draw-the-line tasks before M1. Per the parent level check, playdough, magnet tiles, Lego, crayons, cut cards, clips and rings are fine with a parent present.
- Set it out and play near it. Every card offers two invitations; the child may decline both. Stop immediately on pushback. Never move their hand, supply the answer, or require finishing.
- One printable page and one parent card each day. Name the actual learning decision and a conditional harder rung on both. An invitation choice alone is not the learning decision.
- Synthetic phonics uses sounds, not letter names: initial set s a t p i n; U1's specified dinosaur order is S, T, A. No other taught graphemes yet. Oral blending comes before letter-tile words; no CVC tile reading or tricky words in U1. Tricky words start at R4, two at a time.
- One focal dinosaur per week; its initial is the week's sound. All pages carry the child's name for optional finger exploration, without name-letter testing. No letter formation requirement.
- Diet, era and feature claims need two sources. The weekly dinosaur is the star; the three U1 dinosaurs and T. rex may supply contrasts. No invented colors, plate purpose, or claim these dinosaurs lived together.
- M0 opportunities include dough loops and free crayon circles on scrap paper. Dough and finger play are practice; only an independent rough crayon circle meets M0.
- Exit checks are optional two-minute glimpses during play. Parent cards ask one plain question about what happened; declining is fine and never failure.
- Focal dinosaur and sound: Triceratops; T /t/ (a clipped sound, no added uh). Facts to verify before build: Three horns and a frill; Late Cretaceous; plants.
Open questions:
- ASSUMED: first-sound readiness is unknown; two familiar words are an entry point, not a skill already established. If their names are unfamiliar, first share their names and leave the sound choice for another day.
