# W2-D2 Four dough leaves for Triceratops
Parent: W2 — offer the four-from-eight feeding decision with dough and a magnet-tile pen.
Serves: N1 L1
Goal: Invite the child to prepare four pretend leaves for a plant-eating Triceratops and decide when to stop serving.
- Template: feed-the-dino. Triceratops occupies one side of the page beside a broad blank feeding mat; the other side remains an open serving space with no four slots, counted dots, or prefilled food. A small numeral 4 rides along without requiring identification. Include the child's name. The parent card carries the plant-diet teaching, two invitations, setup, decision, extension, and observation question; repeat decision and harder rung in an adult-facing page footer.
- Decision the child makes: When has they served the requested four leaves from a supply of eight? The open mat does not tell them where to stop.
- Invitation (two alternatives): "Want to make dough leaves for Triceratops, or build their pen before dinner?" Dough food and magnet-tile building are both core ways into play, not rewards.
- Setup/play: 2–10 minutes with a parent, hands first. Set the mat down with dough, eight magnet tiles, and a printed Triceratops picture. Adult and child flatten eight pretend dough leaves; the adult can finish the supply while the child plays, so making eight is not a prerequisite. Say "Triceratops ate plants; these are pretend leaves." Build a pen beside the mat by standing tiles on their edges in a simple open square, leaving room for the picture and a doorway; invite the child to build or change it. Choose the short route they enjoy rather than requiring both constructions. Place all eight leaves together outside the feeding space and ask "Give Triceratops four leaves." Let them serve and stop without adult counting, separated sets, or four place settings. If they prefer to start with three, request three and revisit four later.
- Harder rung: If they independently serves four and wants more, return all eight to the supply and request five. Keep the mat empty of count cues and do not require numeral naming.
- Materials: One feeding page and one parent card; playdough sufficient for eight pretend leaves; washable work surface; eight magnet tiles; one supplied printed Triceratops picture; adult scissors if cutting out the picture. No real food is needed. Parent present for dough, tiles, and cut cards.
Exit check: An optional two-minute glimpse: after returning eight leaves to the supply, ask for four and notice where the child stops without counting for them. Card question: "How many leaves did the child choose to serve before they stopped?" If they only builds or declines, that is fine; leave the number unknown. Naming Triceratops and hearing its diet support L1 without proving the eight-dinosaur milestone.
Children:

- Numbers start with 3–4 objects, stretch to 5 only when ready; dot-to-dot matching starts at 3 then 4. Numerals ride along from W1, never required answers. Subitizing and play checks A, B, D, G, I remain unknown.
- Every activity lasts 2–10 minutes, hands first; no pencils, sitting lessons or draw-the-line tasks before M1. Per the parent level check, playdough, magnet tiles, Lego, crayons, cut cards, clips and rings are fine with a parent present.
- Set it out and play near it. Every card offers two invitations; the child may decline both. Stop immediately on pushback. Never move their hand, supply the answer, or require finishing.
- One printable page and one parent card each day. Name the actual learning decision and a conditional harder rung on both. An invitation choice alone is not the learning decision.
- Synthetic phonics uses sounds, not letter names: initial set s a t p i n; U1's specified dinosaur order is S, T, A. No other taught graphemes yet. Oral blending comes before letter-tile words; no CVC tile reading or tricky words in U1. Tricky words start at R4, two at a time.
- One focal dinosaur per week; its initial is the week's sound. All pages carry the child's name for optional finger exploration, without name-letter testing. No letter formation requirement.
- Diet, era and feature claims need two sources. The weekly dinosaur is the star; the three U1 dinosaurs and T. rex may supply contrasts. No invented colors, plate purpose, or claim these dinosaurs lived together.
- M0 opportunities include dough loops and free crayon circles on scrap paper. Dough and finger play are practice; only an independent rough crayon circle meets M0.
- Exit checks are optional two-minute glimpses during play. Parent cards ask one plain question about what happened; declining is fine and never failure.
- Focal dinosaur and sound: Triceratops; T /t/ (a clipped sound, no added uh). Facts to verify before build: Three horns and a frill; Late Cretaceous; plants.
