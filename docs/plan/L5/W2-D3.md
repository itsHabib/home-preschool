# W2-D3 Who ate something different?
Parent: W2 — teach four dinosaur diets before an uncued recall choice and a one-picture hiding game.
Serves: L1
Goal: Invite the child to recall which dinosaur ate meat after hearing each dinosaur's diet.
- Template: odd-one-out. Four equal unmarked panels show Stegosaurus, Triceratops, Ankylosaurus, and T. rex in the same line-art style; Triceratops remains the weekly star in the title. No food icons, diet captions, matching colors, category marks, or exaggerated visual answer cues appear in the choice grid. Include the child's name. The parent card holds the four adult-read diet facts out of the child’s choice view, instructions to cut the page into four cards after teaching if wanted, decision, invitation, extension, and observation question. Repeat decision and harder rung in an adult-facing footer that can be folded out of view.
- Decision the child makes: Which of the four named dinosaurs ate meat instead of plants, based on the facts they have heard? Shape helps distinguish identities but does not establish diet.
- Invitation (two alternatives): "Want to help tell the dinosaurs' dinner story, or play a picture-hiding game after their dinner story?" They may choose either or neither.
- Setup/play: 2–10 minutes with a parent, hands first. Lay out the four pictures and introduce each with the card's verified facts: Stegosaurus ate plants; Triceratops ate plants; Ankylosaurus ate plants; T. rex ate meat. Invite names and conversation while sharing these facts; do not ask the child to infer diets from horns, plates, armor, or teeth. Put the parent fact card out of view and spend a little time arranging or handling the pictures. Later, if they are interested, shuffle their positions and ask "Which one ate meat?" They can point or place it apart; no circling required. Accept words or gestures and explain again after the choice if useful. Then offer one short hiding turn: name the four together, invite them to close their eyes or look away, cover one with a cloth, and ask which picture is hiding; reveal it whenever they wishe. Neither game requires completion.
- Harder rung: If the diet choice and hiding turn are enjoyable, invite them to name the hidden dinosaur and recall what it ate before revealing it. Do not add more pictures or claim a T2 milestone check.
- Materials: One four-picture page and one parent card with all four diet facts; adult scissors to cut the four panels if they choose movable cards; a small cloth large enough to cover one whole panel. Use supplied printed dinosaurs until toy identities are known; parent present for cut cards.
Exit check: Optional two-minute glimpse later in play, with the fact card out of view: shuffle the pictures and ask which dinosaur ate meat, without naming it or gesturing. Card question: "Which dinosaur did the child pick, and what did they say about its food?" A point counts as a response; no reason or full sentence is required. If they decline or the facts were just repeated as a cue, enjoy the story and leave independent recall unknown.
Children:
Constraints inherited:
- Starting calibration, from the first level check: "Counting things | 1–4 with meaning"; "Letters | Does not recognise letters yet, including their own name"; "Drawing | Cannot draw a circle yet"; "Temperament | Frustrated when they feel forced".
- Numbers start with 3–4 objects, stretch to 5 only when ready; dot-to-dot matching starts at 3 then 4. Numerals ride along from W1, never required answers. Subitizing and play checks A, B, D, G, I remain unknown.
- Every activity lasts 2–10 minutes, hands first; no pencils, sitting lessons or draw-the-line tasks before M1. Per the parent level check, playdough, magnet tiles, Lego, crayons, cut cards, clips and rings are fine with a parent present.
- Set it out and play near it. Every card offers two invitations; the child may decline both. Stop immediately on pushback. Never move their hand, supply the answer, or require finishing.
- One printable page and one parent card each day. Name the actual learning decision and a conditional harder rung on both. An invitation choice alone is not the learning decision.
- Synthetic phonics uses sounds, not letter names: initial set s a t p i n; U1's specified dinosaur order is S, T, A. No other taught graphemes yet. Oral blending comes before letter-tile words; no CVC tile reading or tricky words in U1. Tricky words start at R4, two at a time.
- One focal dinosaur per week; its initial is the week's sound. All pages carry the child's name for optional finger exploration, without name-letter testing. No letter formation requirement.
- Diet, era and feature claims need two sources. The weekly dinosaur is the star; the three U1 dinosaurs and T. rex may supply contrasts. No invented colors, plate purpose, or claim these dinosaurs lived together.
- M0 opportunities include dough loops and free crayon circles on scrap paper. Dough and finger play are practice; only an independent rough crayon circle meets M0.
- Exit checks are optional two-minute glimpses during play. Parent cards ask one plain question about what happened; declining is fine and never failure.
- Focal dinosaur and sound: Triceratops; T /t/ (a clipped sound, no added uh). Facts to verify before build: Three horns and a frill; Late Cretaceous; plants.
Open questions:
- ASSUMED: the four diets are new learning unless the child shows otherwise. Teach them before recall; confirm all four diet claims against two sources in the parent build pass. Remembering four hidden pictures is not assumed or required; reveal promptly if the hiding game is not fun.
