# W1-D2 Match the eggs and build a pen
Parent: W1 — strengthen meaningful groups of three and four using egg cards, dough eggs, and magnet tiles.
Serves: N1
Goal: Decide which egg card has the same amount, then explore that amount through one short hands-first route lasting 2–10 minutes.
  Template: count-and-match; portrait US Letter with the child's name and a fixed Stegosaurus header, then four cut-card panels: two show three eggs in different arrangements and two show four eggs in different arrangements. Egg sizes stay consistent; no pair shares an answer color, numbered label, connector, or unique border. A tiny separate adult reference strip shows 3 and 4 for optional numeral exposure after matching. The adult footer and parent card state the decision and harder rung.
  Decision the child makes: Which card has the same number of eggs as the target?
  Invitation (two alternatives): “Want to make dough eggs for Stegosaurus, or build its egg pen with magnet tiles?” Both are starting routes, not rewards for finishing the cards.
  Setup/play: Cut four egg cards; keep the numeral strip aside. Dough, mat and eight magnet tiles nearby. While they make dough eggs, join four tiles into a pen; if they build the pen, make the eggs. Then put a three-egg card above two choices (three and four); set the spare card aside. Let them find the same amount. Shuffle choices and switch the target to four when they want another go. Fill the pen with the matching number; tile count does not matter.
  Harder rung: Empty the pen, gather eight dough eggs outside it, and invite five if four was easy.
  Materials: One activity page, one parent card, adult scissors, playdough sufficient for eight eggs, washable mat, and eight magnet tiles. Use the printed Stegosaurus header until toy identity is known; parent present throughout.
Exit check: For an optional two-minute glimpse, offer a pile of eight dough eggs and invite them to put three in the pen, or four if three was already easy. The card asks: “How many eggs did the child put in before stopping?” Note the request and what happened, including “not today.” Do not count for them or correct the set as part of the observation; matching success alone is not a numeral or subitizing result.
Children:
Constraints inherited:
- Calibration: counts 1–4 with meaning; does not recognize letters, including their own name; cannot yet draw a circle; gets frustrated when forced.
- Every activity lasts 2–10 minutes, hands first; no pencils, sitting lessons or draw-the-line tasks before M1. Per the parent level check, playdough, magnet tiles, Lego, crayons, cut cards, clips and rings are fine with a parent present.
- Set it out and play near it. Every card offers two invitations; the child may decline both. Stop immediately on pushback. Never move their hand, supply the answer, or require finishing.
- One printable page and one parent card each day. Name the actual learning decision and a conditional harder rung on both. An invitation choice alone is not the learning decision.
- Numbers begin at 3–4; stretch to 5 only when ready. Numerals ride along without required recognition. Subitizing remains unknown.
- Sounds, not letter names; U1 introduces only s/t/a. No CVC tile reading or tricky words. The child's name is on every page for optional finger exploration; no name-letter testing or required letter formation.
- Diet, era and feature claims need two sources. Stegosaurus is the star; the three U1 dinosaurs and T. rex may supply contrasts. No invented colors, plate purpose, or claim these dinosaurs lived together.
- M0 opportunities include dough loops and free crayon circles on scrap paper. Dough and finger play are practice; only an independent rough crayon circle meets M0.
- Exit checks are optional two-minute glimpses during play. Parent cards ask one plain question about what happened; declining is fine and never failure.
Open questions:
- ASSUMED: subitizing and numeral recognition are unknown; countable three/four egg groups are the starting route, with numeral exposure optional.
