# W3-D4 Ankylosaurus Stegosaurus next
Parent: W3 — Give W3 a one-gap AB choice with dinosaur pictures and magnet tiles as core play routes.
Serves: T1
Goal: Invite the child to choose the next item in a repeating dinosaur or magnet-tile sequence.
- Template: pattern-strip; one portrait US Letter page with a horizontal row Ankylosaurus, Stegosaurus, Ankylosaurus, Stegosaurus, one blank cell. Below it provide two adult-cut choice cards: one Ankylosaurus and one Stegosaurus, all equal size and neutral background. Keep the strip attached; the child's name appears in the footer. No second baseline gap, answer silhouette or prompt-colored cell. The parent card and adult-facing footer repeat the decision, invitations, extension and observation. The dinosaur comparison is a picture game, not a claim they lived together.
- Decision the child makes: Decide which of the two dinosaur pictures, or which of two tile colors, continues the repeating AB order.
- Invitation (two alternatives): "Shall we make a magnet-tile pattern, or give the dinosaur parade its next picture?" the child can decline both.
- Setup/play: 2–10 minutes with a parent present. Adult cuts only the two choice cards and sets out four red and four blue magnet tiles. Start with a short demonstration away from the final turn: lay red-blue-red-blue and show the next red while naming the colors, then dismantle it. For tile play, make a fresh blue-red-blue-red row with one next position and offer one blue and one red tile equally close. For dinosaur play, offer the printed Ankylosaurus-Stegosaurus-Ankylosaurus-Stegosaurus row and one card of each dinosaur side by side. They may point or place, and the adult can name the pictures without naming the answer. Give one uncued next-item turn after teaching; leave it there unless they want more. Magnet-tile building is available from the start, not earned by solving the page.
- Harder rung: After the first tile choice, offer one more position with both colors replenished. The printed row keeps one gap; use floor tiles for this extension.
- Materials: One printable page yielding an attached one-gap strip and two dinosaur choice cards, one parent card, adult scissors, four red and four blue magnet tiles. Parent stays present for cutting and tile play; no extra blank card is needed for the optional position beside the strip.
Exit check: Optional, within two minutes after shared demonstration: the parent card asks "What did the child choose to come next?" Present one fresh ABAB row with both possible next items available and no adult answer cue. Notice their chosen item, pointing or placement. Demonstration copying and adult help are shared practice; a decline leaves readiness unknown.
Children:
Constraints inherited:
- Starting calibration, from the first level check: "Counting things | 1–4 with meaning"; "Letters | Does not recognise letters yet, including their own name"; "Drawing | Cannot draw a circle yet"; "Temperament | Frustrated when they feel forced".
- Numbers start with 3–4 objects, stretch to 5 only when ready; dot-to-dot matching starts at 3 then 4. Numerals ride along from W1, never required answers. Subitizing and play checks A, B, D, G, I remain unknown.
- Every activity lasts 2–10 minutes, hands first; no pencils, sitting lessons or draw-the-line tasks before M1. Per the parent level check, playdough, magnet tiles, Lego, crayons, cut cards, clips and rings are fine with a parent present.
- Set it out and play near it. Every card offers two invitations; the child may decline both. Stop immediately on pushback. Never move their hand, supply the answer, or require finishing.
- One printable page and one parent card each day. Name the actual learning decision and a conditional harder rung on both. An invitation choice alone is not the learning decision.
- Synthetic phonics uses sounds, not letter names: initial set s a t p i n; U1's specified dinosaur order is S, T, A. No other taught graphemes yet. Oral blending comes before letter-tile words; no CVC tile reading or tricky words in U1. Tricky words start at R4, two at a time.
- One focal dinosaur per week; its initial is the week's sound. All pages carry the child's name for optional finger exploration, without name-letter testing. No letter formation requirement.
- Diet, era and feature claims need two sources. The weekly dinosaur is the star; the three U1 dinosaurs and T. rex may supply contrasts. No invented colors, plate purpose, or claim these dinosaurs lived together.
- M0 opportunities include dough loops and free crayon circles on scrap paper. Dough and finger play are practice; only an independent rough crayon circle meets M0.
- Exit checks are optional two-minute glimpses during play. Parent cards ask one plain question about what happened; declining is fine and never failure.
- Focal dinosaur and sound: Ankylosaurus; A /a/ as in apple and ankle. Facts to verify before build: Bony armor and a tail club; Late Cretaceous; plants.
Open questions:
- ASSUMED: Pattern readiness is unknown. Start with shared demonstration and one fresh gap; a second gap belongs only to the extension, with both choices replenished. Dinosaur art and facts remain subject to the parent verification.
