# W2-D4 Two dinosaurs, two colors
Parent: W2 — offer Stegosaurus/Triceratops AB and a two-color magnet-tile AB route.
Serves: T1
Goal: Invite the child to continue an alternating dinosaur or magnet-tile pattern without an answer cue.
- Template: pattern-strip. The page shows four equal cells containing Stegosaurus, Triceratops, Stegosaurus, Triceratops, followed by one blank cell; a lower cut band contains one Stegosaurus and one Triceratops candidate card, with no matching colors or highlighted answer. Include the child's name and identify Triceratops as the weekly star outside the row. The parent card carries both the dinosaur and tile routes, invitation, decision, extension, and observation question; repeat decision and harder rung in an adult-facing page footer.
- Decision the child makes: Which dinosaur or tile color continues the AB sequence? The invitation and preferred color do not count as solving the pattern.
- Invitation (two alternatives): "Want to finish a dinosaur parade, or build a two-color tile road?" Both are core routes; choose one short game rather than requiring both.
- Setup/play: 2–10 minutes with a parent, hands first. Set out four red and four blue magnet tiles plus the page; adult cuts the two candidate cards. ASSUMED: AB readiness is unknown. First demonstrate a short alternating tile road, saying each color as you place it, then clear the example. For tile play, lay red-blue-red-blue and offer a red and blue candidate without completing the chant; ask "What comes next?" For dinosaur play, name the two dinosaurs, point along the printed row without supplying the fifth name, and offer the two cut cards. The child may place a card or point. Keep the two candidate positions changeable; no pointing, leaning, or color cue to the answer. Stop or return to shared building if the choice is not enjoyable.
- Harder rung: After the first tile choice, offer one more position with both colors replenished. The printed row keeps one gap; use floor tiles for this extension.
- Materials: One pattern page and one parent card; four red and four blue magnet tiles of matching shape; adult scissors for the two candidate cards; a clear floor space. If these exact colors are unavailable, choose any two visibly different colors and use those names consistently. Parent present for tiles and cut cards.
Exit check: Optional two-minute glimpse: make a fresh red-blue-red-blue row or reuse the dinosaur row, swap the candidate positions from the earlier turn, and ask what comes next once. Card question: "What did the child add to the pattern on their own?" Record the object or color if useful; a shared building turn or decline is fine and leaves independent continuation unknown.
Children:
Constraints inherited:
- Starting calibration, from the first level check: "Counting things | 1–4 with meaning"; "Letters | Does not recognise letters yet, including their own name"; "Drawing | Cannot draw a circle yet"; "Temperament | Frustrated when they feel forced".
- Numbers start with 3–4 objects, stretch to 5 only when ready; dot-to-dot matching starts at 3 then 4. Numerals ride along from W1, never required answers. Subitizing and play checks A, B, D, G, I remain unknown.
- Every activity lasts 2–10 minutes, hands first; no pencils, sitting lessons or draw-the-line tasks before M1. Per the parent level check, playdough, magnet tiles, Lego, crayons, cut cards, clips and rings are fine with a parent present.
- Set it out and play near it. Every card offers two invitations; the child may decline both. Stop immediately on pushback. Never move their hand, supply the answer, or require finishing.
- One printable page and one parent card each day. Name the actual learning decision and a conditional harder rung on both. An invitation choice alone is not the learning decision.
- Synthetic phonics uses sounds, not letter names: initial set s a t p i n; U1's specified dinosaur order is S, T, A. No other taught graphemes yet. Oral blending comes before letter-tile words; no CVC tile reading or tricky words in U1. Tricky words start at R4, two at a time.
- One focal dinosaur per week; its initial is the week's sound. All pages carry the child's name for optional finger exploration, without name-letter testing. No letter formation requirement.
- Diet, era and feature claims need two sources. The weekly dinosaur is the star; the three U1 dinosaurs and T. rex may supply contrasts. No invented colors, plate purpose, or claim these dinosaurs lived together.
- M0 opportunities include dough loops and free crayon circles on scrap paper. Dough and finger play are practice; only an independent rough crayon circle meets M0.
- Exit checks are optional two-minute glimpses during play. Parent cards ask one plain question about what happened; declining is fine and never failure.
- Focal dinosaur and sound: Triceratops; T /t/ (a clipped sound, no added uh). Facts to verify before build: Three horns and a frill; Late Cretaceous; plants.
Open questions:
- ASSUMED: pattern readiness is unknown; the demonstration is teaching, and the fresh uncued turn is only an optional observation. The page and toy route do not assume they can name colors or dinosaurs to place a match.
