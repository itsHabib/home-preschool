# W3-D2 Egg cards and a tile pen
Parent: W3 — Give W3 meaningful sets of three and four through dough eggs, matching cards and a magnet-tile pen.
Serves: N1
Goal: Invite the child to match the amount of dough eggs in their pen to an egg card.
- Template: count-and-match; one portrait US Letter page divided into four adult-cut quarter-page cards: three eggs, four eggs, three eggs and four eggs, using different arrangements for equal quantities. Show the same egg design and neutral border on every card; no color or matching-position clues. Each has a small incidental numeral, the child's name and a tiny Ankylosaurus visitor. The parent card and adult-facing page footer repeat the decision, invitations, extension and observation; matching uses placement, never drawn lines.
- Decision the child makes: Which card has the same number of eggs as the target?
- Invitation (two alternatives): "Shall we make the dough eggs first, or build their magnet-tile pen first?" the child can decline both.
- Setup/play: Cut four egg cards; keep the numeral strip aside. Dough, mat and eight magnet tiles nearby. While they make dough eggs, join four tiles into a pen; if they build the pen, make the eggs. Then put a three-egg card above two choices (three and four); set the spare card aside. Let them find the same amount. Shuffle choices and switch the target to four when they want another go. Fill the pen with the matching number; tile count does not matter.
- Harder rung: Empty the pen, gather eight dough eggs outside it, and invite five if four was easy.
- Materials: One printable page yielding four egg cards, one parent card, adult scissors, enough playdough for thirteen pretend eggs for the optional extension, a dough mat and eight magnet tiles. Parent remains present for dough, cutting and building.
Exit check: Optional, within two minutes while they play: the parent card asks "Which card did the child choose for their four eggs?" Set out four eggs and the three/four choices without first counting or pointing to an answer. Notice their choice and whether they counted or moved the eggs. Shared help is welcome but is not an independent response; declining is fine.
Children:
Constraints inherited:
- Starting calibration, from the first level check: "Counting things | 1–4 with meaning"; "Letters | Does not recognise letters yet, including their own name"; "Drawing | Cannot draw a circle yet"; "Temperament | Frustrated when they feel forced".
- Numbers start with 3–4 objects, stretch to 5 only when ready; dot-to-dot matching starts at 3 then 4. Numerals ride along from W1, never required answers. Subitizing and play checks A, B, D, G, I remain unknown.
- Every activity lasts 2–10 minutes, hands first; no pencils, sitting lessons or draw-the-line tasks before M1. Per the parent level check, playdough, magnet tiles, Lego, crayons, cut cards, clips and rings are fine with a parent present.
- Set it out and play near it. Every card offers two invitations; the child may decline both. Stop immediately on pushback. Never move their hand, supply the answer, or require finishing.
- One printable page and one parent card each day. Name the actual learning decision and a conditional harder rung on both. An invitation choice alone is not the learning decision.
- Synthetic phonics uses sounds, not letter names: initial set s a t p i n; U1's specified dinosaur order is S, T, A. No other taught graphemes yet. Oral blending comes before letter-tile words; no CVC tile reading or tricky words in U1. Tricky words start at R4, two at a time.
- One focal dinosaur per week; its initial is the week's sound. All pages carry the child's name for optional finger exploration, without name-letter testing. No letter formation requirement.
- Diet, era and feature claims need two sources. The weekly dinosaur is the star; the three U1 dinosaurs and T. rex may supply contrasts. No invented colors, plate purpose, or claim these dinosaurs lived together.
- M0 opportunities include dough loops and free crayon circles on scrap paper. Dough and finger play are practice; only an independent rough crayon circle meets M0.
- Exit checks are optional two-minute glimpses during play. Parent cards ask one plain question about what happened; declining is fine and never failure.
- Focal dinosaur and sound: Ankylosaurus; A /a/ as in apple and ankle. Facts to verify before build: Bony armor and a tail club; Late Cretaceous; plants.
Open questions:
- ASSUMED: Matching readiness and subitizing remain unknown. Start at three with touch-counting available, then four; five is conditional. Printed numerals are exposure, not a new required task.
