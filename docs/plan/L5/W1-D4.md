# W1-D4 Leaves, eggs, and tile patterns
Parent: W1 — introduce AB alternation through magnet-tile play and a fresh leaf/egg choice.
Serves: T1
Goal: Explore a two-color tile pattern and decide what comes next on a leaf/egg strip in 2–10 minutes of play.
  Template: pattern-strip; portrait US Letter page with a horizontal activity area, the child's name and a fixed Stegosaurus header, a row leaf / egg / leaf / egg / blank, and two detachable choice cards below (one leaf, one egg). Keep both choice cards equally sized and the blank neutral, with no faint answer or matching outline. Adult-facing footer and separate parent card name the continuation decision and harder rung.
  Decision the child makes: Choose leaf or egg for the blank by noticing what alternates; in tile play, choose the next color rather than placing a color the adult names.
  Invitation (two alternatives): “Want to make a two-color magnet-tile road, or finish the leaf-and-egg picture road?” Both routes are core play and either may be declined.
  Setup/play: Adult cuts off the two choice cards and sets the mat beside four red and four blue magnet tiles. Before any uncued turn, demonstrate an AB example on the floor with your own tiles: red / blue / red / blue, then add red while saying what repeats. Let the child touch and rearrange the tiles. Take the demonstrated tile row apart before setting up the fresh turn. For a fresh tile turn, build blue / red / blue / red and offer one loose tile of each color without naming the next one. For a picture turn, show leaf / egg / leaf / egg / blank and offer both cards in shuffled positions; name the pictures naturally first if unfamiliar, then ask “What comes next?” without reciting a hinted answer or moving their hand. Choose one route; the other can wait.
  Harder rung: After the first tile choice, offer one more position with both colors replenished. The printed row keeps one gap; use floor tiles for this extension.
  Materials: One activity page, one parent card, adult scissors, four red and four blue magnet tiles. Substitute two clearly distinguishable available colors and keep the same A/B order. Parent stays present; no extra dinosaur toy is needed.
Exit check: During one optional turn lasting at most two minutes, observe their first choice for the fresh tile row or printed row. The card asks: “Which piece did the child choose next?” Record the row and choice, any help, or “not today.” A response after a hint is practice; one uncued continuation is a useful glimpse, not a mastery claim.
Children:
Constraints inherited:
- Calibration: counts 1–4 with meaning; does not recognize letters, including their own name; cannot yet draw a circle; gets frustrated when forced.
- Every activity lasts 2–10 minutes, hands first; no pencils, sitting lessons or draw-the-line tasks before M1. Per the parent level check, playdough, magnet tiles, Lego, crayons, cut cards, clips and rings are fine with a parent present.
- Set it out and play near it. Every card offers two invitations; the child may decline both. Stop immediately on pushback. Never move their hand, supply the answer, or require finishing.
- One printable page and one parent card each day. Name the actual learning decision and a conditional harder rung on both. An invitation choice alone is not the learning decision.
- Numbers begin at 3–4; stretch to 5 only when ready. Numerals ride along without required recognition. Subitizing remains unknown.
- Sounds, not letter names; U1 introduces only s/t/a. No CVC tile reading or tricky words. The child's name is on every page for optional finger exploration; no name-letter testing or required letter formation.
- Diet, era and feature claims need two sources. Stegosaurus is the star; the three U1 dinosaurs and T. rex may supply contrasts. No invented colors, plate purpose, or claim these dinosaurs lived together.
- M0 opportunities include dough loops and free crayon circles on scrap paper. Dough and finger play are practice; only an independent rough crayon circle meets M0.
- Exit checks are optional two-minute glimpses during play. Parent cards ask one plain question about what happened; declining is fine and never failure.
Open questions:
- ASSUMED: pattern readiness is unknown; begin with the demonstrated example and observe the fresh choice without expecting success.
