# W1-D3 Find Stegosaurus
Parent: W1 — help the child identify and name Stegosaurus among U1 dinosaur shapes.
Serves: L1
Goal: Match Stegosaurus to its silhouette and enjoy naming it through 2–10 minutes of hands-first hide-and-find play.
  Template: shadow-match; portrait US Letter with the child's name and three equally sized silhouette areas for Stegosaurus, Triceratops, and Ankylosaurus across the upper activity area, plus one detachable Stegosaurus picture panel below. Show distinct plates/tail spikes, horns/frill, and armor/club tail; do not label targets or cue the match with color, borders, or size. The Stegosaurus picture and its silhouette share a clear pose. Adult-facing footer and separate parent card name the shape decision and harder rung.
  Decision the child makes: Choose which of the three silhouettes belongs to the Stegosaurus picture, using its shape. Naming the dinosaur and its plant diet are supported conversation, not an immediate recall requirement.
  Invitation (two alternatives): “Want to slide Stegosaurus onto its shadow, or hide its picture for me to find?” Begin with whichever they choose; both may be declined.
  Setup/play: Adult cuts off only the Stegosaurus panel, leaving the three silhouettes on one mat. Set out the mat, picture, and two cloths on the floor. Let the child hold the picture while you say “Stegosaurus”; then place it below the targets and invite a match without pointing out the answer's features. After their turn, notice the back plates and tail spikes together and say it ate plants. For hide-and-find, let them watch you place the picture under one of the two cloths, then invite them to find it; switch roles if they prefer hiding. On finding it, warmly greet “Stegosaurus!” and invite whatever name or comment they offer. This is playful naming, not the four-item missing-game milestone.
  Harder rung: If they easily matches and wants more, turn the detachable picture face down and ask them to find Stegosaurus among the three silhouettes and tell or show what helped. Accept a pointed feature or name approximation.
  Materials: One activity page, one parent card, adult scissors, and two cloths. The supplied printed dinosaurs suffice; no toy inventory or additional cards are needed. Parent present.
Exit check: During an optional two-minute turn, let them find the picture without a fresh naming model. The card asks: “What did the child call the dinosaur they found?” Note their word, gesture, a prompted response, or “not today.” If they volunteer its diet, include it; identifying one dinosaur does not establish L1's eight-dinosaur milestone.
Children:
Constraints inherited:
- Calibration: counts 1–4 with meaning; does not recognize letters, including their own name; cannot yet draw a circle; gets frustrated when forced.
- Every activity lasts 2–10 minutes, hands first; no pencils, sitting lessons or draw-the-line tasks before M1. Per the parent level check, playdough, magnet tiles, Lego, crayons, cut cards, clips and rings are fine with a parent present.
- Set it out and play near it. Every card offers two invitations; the child may decline both. Stop immediately on pushback. Never move their hand, supply the answer, or require finishing.
- One printable page and one parent card each day. Name the actual learning decision and a conditional harder rung on both. An invitation choice alone is not the learning decision.
- Numbers begin at 3–4; stretch to 5 only when ready. Numerals ride along without required recognition. Subitizing remains unknown.
- Sounds, not letter names; U1 introduces only s/t/a. No CVC tile reading or tricky words. The child's name is on every page for optional finger exploration; no name-letter testing or required letter formation.
- Diet, era and feature claims need two sources. Stegosaurus is the star; the three U1 dinosaurs and T. rex may supply contrasts. No invented colors, plate purpose, or claim these dinosaurs lived together.
- M0 opportunities include dough loops and free crayon circles on scrap paper. Dough and finger play are practice; only an independent rough crayon circle meets M0.
- Exit checks are optional two-minute glimpses during play. Parent cards ask one plain question about what happened; declining is fine and never failure.
Open questions:
- Confirm each silhouette and Stegosaurus plant-diet/features in the parent build pass. Printed contrasts do not imply these dinosaurs lived together; toy identities remain unknown.
