# W1-D5 A little Stegosaurus story
Parent: W1 — revisit Stegosaurus vocabulary, /s/, printed S, and round play in a shared adult-read book.
Serves: R1 R2 L1 M0
Goal: Share a four-panel activity book, an oral sound choice, and round play through one chosen 2–10-minute route.
  Template: mini-book; portrait US Letter with four adult-cut panels numbered 1–4. Panel 1: dinosaur portrait, name and isolated S. Panel 2: dinosaur with a leaf visibly at its mouth, caption “It eats plants.” Panel 3: isolated S, familiar sock/ball pictures and adult-read question “Which starts with /s/?” Panel 4: a small dinosaur, “A round resting place,” and blank play space. This is a short shared activity book, not a four-event story or a decodable book. The child's name appears in each panel. Parent reads all captions. No written picture labels, tracing guides or required letter formation. Footer gives decision and extension.
  Decision the child makes: Which picture word starts with /s/: ball or sock?
  Invitation (two alternatives): “Want to turn the little book's pages while I read, or make a dough loop for story time?” Either route can include listening; no need to finish all four pages or every activity.
  Setup/play: Cut the four panels and stack 1–4. Set out dough, a mat, crayons and scrap paper. Read the captions as they turn pages; they are not expected to read them. Talk about the dinosaur eating plants. Name “ball” and “sock” naturally before asking which starts with /s/. Show S and model the sound. On the last page, they can join a dough snake into a resting loop, or make their own crayon circle on scrap. No need to do every part. If they want a repeat, reverse the spoken word order.
  Harder rung: Cover the pictures and invite the sound for S, if they want another turn.
  Repeat play: Reverse the spoken word order on a willing repeat; speak both words naturally. Printed sound choices vary sides across weeks.
  Materials: One page and parent card, adult scissors, optional clip/ring, dough, washable mat or protector, crayons and scrap paper; parent present. Printed sound pictures supply the choices; real objects are not required.
Exit check: Optional two-minute glimpse during willing play. The card asks: “Did they make a crayon circle with the ends meeting?” Notice only a circle they choose to make; do not add a task to collect a result. Dough loops and finger rounds are practice, not M0 attainment. Adult reading or echoed sounds do not establish independent reading. Declining is fine.
Children:
Constraints inherited:
- Calibration: counts 1–4 with meaning; does not recognize letters, including their own name; cannot yet draw a circle; gets frustrated when forced.
- Every activity lasts 2–10 minutes, hands first; no pencils, sitting lessons or draw-the-line tasks before M1. Per the parent level check, playdough, magnet tiles, Lego, crayons, cut cards, clips and rings are fine with a parent present.
- Set it out and play near it. Every card offers two invitations; the child may decline both. Stop immediately on pushback. Never move their hand, supply the answer, or require finishing.
- One printable page and one parent card each day. Name the actual learning decision and a conditional harder rung on both. An invitation choice alone is not the learning decision.
- Numbers begin at 3–4; stretch to 5 only when ready. Numerals ride along without required recognition. Subitizing remains unknown.
- Sounds, not letter names; U1 introduces only s/t/a. No CVC tile reading or tricky words. The child's name is on every page for optional finger exploration; no name-letter testing or required letter formation.
- Diet, era and feature claims need two sources. Stegosaurus is the star; the three U1 dinosaurs and T. rex may supply contrasts. No invented colors, plate purpose, or claim these dinosaurs lived together.
- M0 opportunities include dough loops and free crayon circles on scrap paper. Dough and finger play are practice; only an independent rough crayon circle meets M0.
- Exit checks are optional two-minute glimpses during play. Parent cards ask one plain question about what happened; declining is fine and never failure.
Open questions:
- ASSUMED: first-sound readiness is unknown; begin with the two familiar spoken words sock/ball. Parent build pass verifies plant diet and art; the story is adult-read, not a test of written words.
