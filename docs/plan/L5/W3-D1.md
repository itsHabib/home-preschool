# W3-D1 A sound and a dough loop
Parent: W3 — Offer W3 an oral /a/ choice and independent round play beside Ankylosaurus.
Serves: R1 R2 M0
Goal: Invite the child to hear /a/ and make something round while exploring their dinosaur.
- Template: coloring-page; one portrait US Letter page with a full, large Ankylosaurus outline, A near the heading, a bottom fact strip reading "Ankylosaurus ate plants," and the child's name in the footer. Leave the outline open for optional coloring; no tracing path or picture answer to the sound question. The parent card holds the oral apple/sun choice, decision, two invitations, one extension and observation. Repeat the decision and extension in a small adult-facing footer on the page.
- Decision the child makes: Which spoken word starts with /a/: apple or sun?
- Invitation (two alternatives): "Shall we make a dough loop beside Ankylosaurus, or color its picture first?" the child can decline both.
- Setup/play: The coloring page, crayons and scrap paper; a little dough on a washable mat. Share the dinosaur strip as you play. Let them color or join the ends of a dough snake. A free crayon circle on scrap is welcome too. For a sound turn, say “apple” and “sun” naturally; ask which starts with /a/. If the words or game are new, show one together first. Point to A and model /a/; an echo is welcome. If they want a repeat, reverse the spoken word order.
- Harder rung: If that sound game is easy, try a fresh spoken pair: leaf or ankle.
- Materials: One printable page, one parent card, playdough, a playdough mat, a fat crayon and blank scrap paper. Parent stays present; no additional picture cards or known toy identity are needed.
Exit check: Optional, within two minutes of play: the parent card asks "What did the child pick when you said apple and sun and asked for /a/?" Notice an uncued spoken or pointed response, if they want a turn. On another day the same brief observation can be an independent free crayon circle, noting whether it roughly closes; dough loops are M0 practice. Declining is fine and leaves readiness unknown.
Children:
Constraints inherited:
- Starting calibration, from the first level check: "Counting things | 1–4 with meaning"; "Letters | Does not recognise letters yet, including their own name"; "Drawing | Cannot draw a circle yet"; "Temperament | Frustrated when they feel forced".
- Numbers start with 3–4 objects, stretch to 5 only when ready; dot-to-dot matching starts at 3 then 4. Numerals ride along from W1, never required answers. Subitizing and play checks A, B, D, G, I remain unknown.
- Every activity lasts 2–10 minutes, hands first; no pencils, sitting lessons or draw-the-line tasks before M1. Per the parent level check, playdough, magnet tiles, Lego, crayons, cut cards, clips and rings are fine with a parent present.
- Set it out and play near it. Every card offers two invitations; the child may decline both. Stop immediately on pushback. Never move their hand, supply the answer, or require finishing.
- One printable page and one parent card each day. Name the actual learning decision and a conditional harder rung on both. An invitation choice alone is not the learning decision.
- Synthetic phonics uses sounds, not letter names: initial set s a t p i n; U1's specified dinosaur order is S, T, A. No other taught graphemes yet. Oral blending comes before letter-tile words; no CVC tile reading or tricky words in U1. Tricky words start at R4, two at a time.
- One focal dinosaur per week; its initial is the week's sound. All pages carry the child's name for optional finger exploration, without name-letter testing. No letter formation requirement.
- Diet, era and feature claims need two sources. The weekly dinosaur is the star; the three U1 dinosaurs and T. rex may supply contrasts. No invented colors, plate purpose, or claim these dinosaurs lived together.
- M0 opportunities include dough loops and free crayon circles on scrap paper. Dough and finger play are practice; only an independent rough crayon circle meets M0.
- Exit checks are optional two-minute glimpses during play. Parent cards ask one plain question about what happened; declining is fine and never failure.
- Focal dinosaur and sound: Ankylosaurus; A /a/ as in apple and ankle. Facts to verify before build: Bony armor and a tail club; Late Cretaceous; plants.
Open questions:
- ASSUMED: Familiarity with apple/sun and first-sound readiness; neither is established by the level check. Offer teaching and easy shared play before any independent turn. Diet text and art await the parent fact check.
